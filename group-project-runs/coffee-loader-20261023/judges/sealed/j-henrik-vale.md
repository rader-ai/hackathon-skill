# Signature criterion - Henrik Vale

**Name:** Coffee-Ritual Specificity (the "swap test")

**Description:** Does the motion depict a real, specific moment from making coffee - a bloom swelling as water hits fresh grounds, a crema tiger-striping, a pour-over drawdown, a portafilter tamp, a milk pour folding into a rosetta, beans tumbling in a roaster drum - rather than a stock coffee icon (cup + wavy steam, generic bean) or an abstract spinner tinted brown? I run a literal swap test: mentally replace the coffee artwork with a generic shape. If the animation still reads the same, the idea was never about coffee.

**How I'll score (0-10):**
- **0-2:** Standard spinner/progress bar in coffee colours, or the cup-with-steam cliche. Swapping in a fintech logo changes nothing.
- **3-4:** Recognisably coffee, but only as an icon (cup, bean, mug) with generic motion (rotate, pulse, bounce).
- **5-6:** A real coffee process is referenced, but the motion is generic or the physics are wrong (liquid moves like a progress bar, steam behaves like smoke rings).
- **7-8:** The loop *is* a specific coffee moment, with motion that observes how that material actually behaves (bloom rise and settle, viscous pour, crema spreading). A barista would nod.
- **9-10:** All of the above, and the 5-second loop's structure maps onto the ritual itself (e.g. the reset point is the next shot being pulled), so waiting feels like your coffee being made - and it still reads at 120px.

**Why I care:** Twenty years in hospitality branding have taught me that the category cliche is the enemy. Every coffee app has a steaming cup; none of them own anything. A loader is shown thousands of times a day while a customer waits for their drink - it is the one moment the brand can make the wait feel like craft happening behind the bar. If the idea survives being swapped onto a banking app, it is wallpaper, not a brand asset.
