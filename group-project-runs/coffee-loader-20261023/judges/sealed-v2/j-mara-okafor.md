# Signature Criterion v2 - Mara Okafor

**Name:** One Lead Mover

**Description:** Is there a clear motion hierarchy? At any moment the eye should have exactly one primary mover that carries "working on it", with every other moving element visibly subordinate to it: smaller amplitude, lower contrast change, and on a tempo that nests with the lead (same period or a clean multiple/division), never a competing rhythm. Observable things: (1) how many independently moving elements there are and whether one obviously leads; (2) whether secondary motion has less travel/contrast than the lead; (3) whether any two tempos fight (e.g. a hard 1Hz beat over a slow 5s swell that both demand attention); (4) whether the eye has to jump between two places on the canvas to follow what is happening.

**How I'll score (0-10):**
- Watch 3 loops at 120x120 and at splash size. For each moving element, note travel (or scale/opacity range), contrast change, and period. Then glance away and back ~5 times and note where my eye lands first.
- 0-2: two or more elements compete as lead (different places, comparable contrast, unrelated tempos); my eye lands somewhere different on each glance.
- 3-4: a lead exists but one secondary element matches it in contrast or amplitude, or a second tempo pulls attention (a flashing accent, a counter, a hitch).
- 5-6: one clear lead; secondaries are subordinate but at least one is busier than it needs to be or sits far from the lead, splitting the eye.
- 7-8: one lead, secondaries clearly quieter and on nested tempos; my eye lands on the lead on nearly every glance.
- 9-10: every glance lands on the same mover at both sizes; secondary motion is felt rather than seen and only adds warmth; the reduced-motion version keeps the same single focal point.

**Why I care:** In our delivery-app motion guidelines the first rule was "one thing moves, everything else breathes". Loaders that failed in research were rarely ugly; they were busy. Two movers on two rhythms make a waiting user track instead of rest, and a wait you have to track feels longer. A loader with a single, obvious lead is calmer at a glance, survives being shrunk, and gives a motion system something to build on.
