# Signature criterion v2 - Henrik Vale

**Name:** Freeze-Frame Brandability (the "cup sleeve test")

**Description:** Pause the loader at any moment and print that single frame on a cup sleeve, a bag stamp or a menu board. Does every still frame stand on its own as a composed, ownable brand mark, or do some frames collapse into half-drawn shapes, overlapping blobs, empty fields or something that looks like a rendering error? I sample four frames per loop (0s, 1.25s, 2.5s, 3.75s) and also look for the worst frame I can find by scrubbing. I check each one in two ways: at full size in its own colours, and reduced to a single ink (one colour on kraft paper) at roughly 2cm, the way it would sit on packaging.

**How I'll score (0-10):**
- **0-2:** Most frames are not a mark: mid-motion debris, shapes cut off or overlapping awkwardly, or the frame only makes sense while moving. In one ink it turns into an unreadable smudge or a generic circle.
- **3-4:** The rest pose is a decent mark, but at least two of the four sampled frames look broken or arbitrary, or the mark relies on colour and falls apart in one ink.
- **5-6:** All sampled frames read as the same object, but they look like stills from an animation and not like designed marks; the composition is centred-icon generic, or the worst frame I find is clearly unfinished.
- **7-8:** Every sampled frame is a composed mark with clear silhouette, balance and negative space, and it survives one-ink reduction at 2cm. I could put any of them on a sleeve.
- **9-10:** Every frame works, the worst frame I can find still holds, and the frames form a family: each one is a deliberate variation of the same mark, so the loader doubles as a ready-made set of packaging stamps or menu icons.

**Why I care:** In hospitality the loader never lives alone. The same mark ends up on the sleeve, the bag, the stamp card and the board above the bar, usually as a still and in one ink. Motion that only looks good while it moves is a motion trick; a mark that holds in every frame is a brand asset that the café can print. Twenty years of dielines have taught me that the identity is decided by its worst still, not its best second of animation.
