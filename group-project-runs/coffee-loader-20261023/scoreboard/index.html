Coffee Loader Cupping Table

Three teams of four agents each built a 5-second looping loader for a coffee-ordering app in plain HTML and CSS. Five judges scored them on a shared rubric, plus one criterion each that they kept sealed until the end. Then every judge gave each team one fix, the teams decided which to take, and built a second version. The loaders below are the real builds, running live.

How the points add up

Criterion scores are the average of five judges, out of 10. A team's rubric score is each judge's weighted total, averaged. Sealed criteria add up to 3 points per judge.

Known issues with this run

Isolation leaked. Builders shared a local test port and the in-app browser pane, so two Warm Hands members briefly saw Bare Wire's build. Both say they used nothing from it, and Warm Hands won, so treat the ranking as indicative. The skill now gives each team its own port and a private browser.

Ideas converged early. Nine of twelve first proposals were a drop or a crema ring, likely steered by technique examples in the judges' brief. The Head Judge prompt now describes outcomes only.

Testing was thin. Every build was checked in headless Chromium only. Safari, Firefox and real phones are untested.

Judges drifted between rounds. After writing their notes they graded the same v1 builds lower than in round 1. The rematch change is measured inside one pass to cancel this, but round 1 and rematch totals still aren't on the same scale.
