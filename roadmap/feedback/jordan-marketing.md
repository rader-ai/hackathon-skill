# Jordan Ellis: first-look feedback on Group Project

## 1. Who I am and what I'd use this for

I'm a content and marketing manager. I'm not technical. I'd give it three briefs:

- "Give me 10 taglines for our spring menu launch, in three tones, with one line on why each works."
- "Write a four-email welcome sequence for people who signed up for our newsletter at a trade show."
- "Draft landing-page copy for our catering service: headline, three benefits, one call to action, and a short FAQ."

I need the output as words I can paste into a Google Doc and show my boss.

## 2. First impression

I understood the basic idea: several AI teams compete on one brief, and judges pick a winner. The bakery 404 example made sense because the pages were visual and easy to compare.

Words that confused me: "subagents," "Host," "sealed criteria," "Signature bonus," "Spread," "lite" versus "standard," "TIMEBOX," "tally.py," "Opus/Sonnet/Haiku," and "Rematch." I had to guess that "Spread" meant how much the judges disagreed.

I also couldn't tell whether I'd get copy or a web page. The example delivered a working HTML page with a picture, not a list of options I could read.

## 3. Friction

- **Install.** I'd need to clone a GitHub repo, run `ln -s`, and run `npm install`. I stop at the word "terminal."
- **Starting it.** I'd have to know to type `/group-project` and what to put after it.
- **Waiting.** "About 15 minutes" with no sign of progress unless I watch the whole time.
- **Intake.** The questions about "Medium" and "Mode" would leave me guessing.
- **Output.** I'd get HTML files and PNGs, not a document. Copy would be buried inside code.
- **Renders.** At phone width, the pencil strike in Group C covers the text. Group A's "No." and "4-loaf-4" ticket made me stop and decode it. Those are design notes, but they show the judging looks at pictures, not words.

## 4. What would make me use it every week

- Results as a document, with a plain summary on top: option, why it works, what to watch.
- Brand voice and banned words entered once, then reused on every run.
- One sentence saying what it's doing now and when it will finish.
- A "best three lines to paste" version I can send my boss without editing.
- Plain-English labels: "strongest hook," "safest for our audience," not "Craft & execution: 6.0."

## 5. Ideas backlog

1. **Paste-ready copy pack.** Problem: I can't paste results from a web page. Proposal: export every option, grouped by brief, to a Google Doc or Word file. Value: high.
2. **Brand voice once.** Problem: I re-explain tone and words to avoid every time. Proposal: a short brand-voice file every team reads first. Value: high.
3. **Marketing brief form.** Problem: "Medium" and "Mode" mean nothing to me. Proposal: fill-in fields for audience, goal, channel, tone, length, and must-include facts. Value: high.
4. **Invented-facts check.** Problem: the bakery pages show hours and "Open right now," and I can't tell which came from the brief. Proposal: flag every claim not in my brief so I can accept or delete it. Value: high.
5. **Boss one-pager.** Problem: I need to show a decision, not a scoreboard. Proposal: one page with the winner, why, and two alternates. Value: med.
6. **No-terminal start.** Problem: setup stops me before I start. Proposal: a hosted version or one-click setup. Value: high.
7. **Progress in plain words.** Problem: long waits with no feedback. Proposal: one status line per phase, such as "Writing options, 2 of 3 done." Value: low.

## 6. Deal-breakers

- Output that is code, HTML, or images when I asked for words.
- Facts I didn't supply (hours, stock, claims) that I can't see and remove. For a bakery's public page, invented hours would be a real problem.
- Needing a terminal to start.
- Jargon I can't translate without help.
